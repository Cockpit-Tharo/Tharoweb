## 1. EXECUTIVE SUMMARY

Uncomplic8.tech is a WhatsApp-first business systems studio. Instead of selling businesses one more generic tool they have to learn, Uncomplic8 builds each client a working system — CRM, booking, inventory, staff, billing, whatever their sector needs — with WhatsApp as the front door customers and staff already know how to use, backed by a real website, and found by customers through SEO.

Three things separate this from a typical "WhatsApp API tool" subscription:

- **It's built, not just switched on.** Competitors sell a dashboard and leave the business to configure it. Uncomplic8 sells the finished, sector-specific system — booking flow, reminder sequences, review automation, templates — already built around how that business actually runs.
- **It's one vendor for the whole stack.** Website, SEO, and WhatsApp automation come from the same team, so the WhatsApp "Book Now" button, the Google-ranked landing page, and the automated confirmation message are all part of one design, not three subscriptions duct-taped together.
- **Pricing is transparent where competitors are not.** Research into Wati, AiSensy, and DoubleTick (Section 4) turned up a consistent pattern: attractive advertised prices that don't include per-message WhatsApp charges, per-seat add-ons, or automation-trigger quotas — so real bills run well above the sticker price. Uncomplic8's model separates "what we charge" from "what Meta charges" clearly, from the first conversation.

This document lays out the brand, the 12 industry playbooks, competitor research, a pricing framework, the tech approach, and an operational checklist to get the company officially running.

**What the research actually shows:** all three are genuinely capable platforms with large customer bases — Wati alone reports thousands of brands across dozens of countries — but they are horizontal tools. None of them ship a salon's commission-tracking logic, a dealership's service-due calculation, or a Tally ledger read. That configuration work is left entirely to the business, or to whoever they hire to set it up — which is exactly the gap Uncomplic8 fills.

The other consistent theme across independent reviews and pricing breakdowns of these platforms is **layered, non-obvious cost**: the advertised monthly plan is only the software fee. On top of it sits Meta's own per-message charge, per-seat overage once a team exceeds the included agent count, and per-automation-trigger quotas that get consumed quickly once a business runs real chatbot flows. Several third-party pricing breakdowns calculate real-world bills 30–260% above the advertised "starting at" price once seats, add-ons, and message volume are added in. This is a trust gap Uncomplic8 can close simply by pricing plainly.

### 4.2 The underlying cost layer: Meta's own pricing (this affects every WhatsApp business, regardless of provider)

Since July 2025, Meta bills **per delivered template message** (not per 24-hour conversation as before), split into three categories: Marketing, Utility, and Authentication. Rates vary by recipient country and are revised periodically.

**India rates (as of the most recent update Anthropic's research could confirm, effective January 2026):**

| Category | Approx. rate | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Marketing | ₹0.86 / message | Roughly 7–8× the utility/auth rate — this is what drives cost on broadcast campaigns |
| Utility | ₹0.115 / message | Order confirmations, reminders, status updates |
| Authentication (OTP) | ₹0.115 / message (domestic) | Higher for international authentication |
| Service replies (business-initiated inside the 24-hr customer window) | Free until Sept 30, 2026 | **Becomes chargeable at the utility rate from October 1, 2026** — a real shift worth planning pricing around now |

Two important nuances to build into every client conversation and into Uncomplic8's own cost model:
- **Click-to-WhatsApp ad clicks** (from Meta/Instagram ads) open a **72-hour free messaging window** — a meaningful cost lever for the Lead Management (Section 7.4) and Real Estate (Section 7.9) playbooks, which lean on Meta ad funnels.
- **18% GST** applies on top of Meta's charges for Indian businesses, and most BSPs pass this through directly.

This is exactly the layer competitors bury inside "credits" and "conversation charges." Uncomplic8's pricing model (Section 8) should show it as its own clearly labeled line.

### 4.3 Vertical competitors already exist — know them before pitching
Several sectors already have dedicated software with WhatsApp bolted on. This isn't a blank market — it's a chance to compete on integration quality and bundling, not on being "the first."

- **Salons/spas:** DINGG, Zenoti, Cleomitra, Salon360, AyeSalon, SalonBoost, Snip and Glow — most already offer WhatsApp reminders and booking. Differentiate through deeper commission/staff-payroll logic, review-link tracking, and bundling the salon's own website + local SEO (most of these competitors sell software only, not the website that brings customers in).
- **Tally + WhatsApp:** PickyAssist, WA.Expert, Comhard, Growth Partners, and several TDL-based connectors already do invoice/ledger delivery over WhatsApp. Differentiate by pairing this with a proper CRM layer on top (segmenting debtors, automated 7/15/30-day escalation sequences, marketing broadcasts to the same contact list) rather than one-way document delivery.
- **Logistics:** Shipsy, FarEye, ClickPost, TrackoMile, Track-POD are enterprise-grade and largely sell to large 3PLs; there's real room to serve the mid-market logistics and courier businesses these platforms are priced above.

The honest positioning: Uncomplic8 is not claiming to out-feature category leaders like Zenoti or FarEye for large enterprises. It's the right-sized, WhatsApp-first, website-and-SEO-included option for the much larger population of single-location and small-chain businesses those platforms under-serve or over-charge.

## 9. TECH STACK & DELIVERY MODEL

### 9.1 WhatsApp layer
- **Meta WhatsApp Cloud API**, accessed either directly (Uncomplic8 becomes a Meta Tech Provider over time) or initially through an existing BSP relationship while volume is low — this is a build-vs-buy decision worth revisiting once monthly message volume across clients justifies direct Meta onboarding.
- Template message library managed centrally, with a lightweight internal tool to draft, submit, and track approval status per client (turns the "new templates" pain point from Section 7.1 into a real product feature, not manual work on your end).
- WhatsApp Flows (Meta's native in-chat forms) for structured inputs like bookings, feedback forms, and multi-step qualification — reduces back-and-forth message count, which also reduces per-message cost.

### 9.2 Application layer
- Web front ends: Next.js on Vercel (matches your existing portfolio stack, keeps hosting and deployment consistent across your own site and simple client-facing web components).
- Backend/automation: a lightweight middleware layer (Node.js or Python) handling webhook events from WhatsApp, Tally (ODBC/TDL), Shopify/WooCommerce, and POS systems, orchestrated either with custom code or a workflow tool (e.g., n8n) for faster iteration on new sector flows before hard-coding the stable ones.
- Database: Postgres (via Supabase or a managed instance) for client data, message logs, and dashboards.
- Dashboard: a shared internal admin panel across clients, with client-specific views for their team (staff, bookings, inventory, message logs).

### 9.3 Integration priorities by sector
- Tally: ODBC/TDL-based connector (Section 7.3) — this is the highest-value, most differentiated integration to build first given how few WhatsApp-native competitors offer it well.
- Shopify/WooCommerce: use official app APIs for order/cart webhooks.
- POS (Petpooja/Posist-style): confirm API access at the sales stage per client, since availability varies by POS vendor and plan tier.
- Meta Ads (CTWA): standard Meta integration, most BSPs/direct Cloud API access support this natively.

## 11. GETTING OFFICIALLY REGISTERED — OPERATIONAL CHECKLIST

*This is general information to plan around, not legal or financial advice — confirm specifics with a chartered accountant/company secretary before filing anything.*

- **Choose a business structure**: for an early-stage two/three-person technology company in India, the common choices are a Private Limited Company (best if you plan to raise funds or bring in co-founders/ESOPs later) or an LLP (simpler compliance, still limited liability). A sole proprietorship is the fastest to start but offers no liability separation — usually not recommended once you're billing multiple clients under a company name.
- **Name and trademark check**: confirm "Uncomplic8" isn't already trademarked in your service classes (IT services/software, class 42; advertising/marketing, class 35) via the Indian Trade Marks Registry public search, before investing further in branding.
- **Domain and digital presence**: uncomplic8.tech secured; also worth securing the `.com` and key social handles defensively even if `.tech` is primary.
- **GST registration**: required once turnover crosses the threshold, and generally advisable from day one anyway since B2B clients will expect a GST-compliant invoice, and you'll be paying GST on Meta's own charges as an input.
- **MSME/Udyam registration**: quick, free, and unlocks some government tender eligibility and payment-protection benefits for MSME-to-MSME transactions.
- **Meta Business verification**: register a Meta Business Manager, verify the business, and either partner with an existing BSP initially or begin the Tech Provider application process with Meta directly once you're ready to manage WhatsApp API access in-house.
- **Contracts**: a standard MSA (master service agreement) template covering scope, the setup-fee/monthly-fee/messaging-cost split from Section 8, data handling responsibilities (especially relevant for the healthcare and dental sectors), and AMC terms — worth having a lawyer draft once, then reusing per client.
